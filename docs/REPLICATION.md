# Replication of Pereira, Costa and Rocha (2026)

Distance backbones optimize spreading dynamics and centrality ranks in the sparsification of complex networks (arXiv:2603.14564v2). Generated by `python run.py replicate`.

## Settings
- SI model, 100 runs per seed, 12 seeds (5% most, 5% median, 5% least central by eigenvector centrality), proximity `jaccard`, runs capped at 200 steps (nodes never infected get the cap; the paper's stopping rule is undefined on disconnected graphs).
- beta is **our choice**: calibrated so the median half-infection time on the original graph is about 20 steps (the paper does not state its rule). Values: hospital 0.8977.
- Clipping: none (plain Spearman for rho_t and rho_C, xi unclipped).
- X grid: 0.05 to 1, 20 values, plus each X~ itself. wEffRes uses proximity as Laplacian conductance (the paper does not state its weights).
- X~ is computed three ways because the paper does not say which it used: `method` (each method's smallest connected size; DBS fixed at the metric-backbone size), `network` (the largest per-method value, common to all methods) and `metric` (the metric-backbone size, common to all methods). An AUC claim is consistent only if it holds under all three. Eq. 16 divides by (1 - X~), so a method that disconnects early (large X~) is averaged over an easy range; `method` and `network` therefore flatter W.

## Limits
- With one network the claims are checked as the order of methods only. The paper's Mann-Whitney test needs at least 2 networks (it uses 12), so no p-value is computed here.
- The paper's figure values were not available as text, so only the text claims and Tables 1 and 2 are compared.
- Unknown from the paper: its beta rule, its wEffRes Laplacian weights, whether X~ is per method or per network.
